## Mechanical Keyboard

I have always been extremely fond of typing. I remember going to _Minu-mashi's_ office and sitting down with a typewriter with
discarded paper and I would have a go at it using the typewriter. There was nothing of importance that I would be typing, just
typing for the same of it. This used to happen when my aunt had to work and I also required to be looked at and taken care of.

I think that was the reason when it all started and only after using terminal based applications for close to 13 years, I was
able to realize that I loved typing because at a very young age I was exposed to typewriters. Unfortunately, the typing is
restricted to writing code and blog posts like this one only.

The days of typewriters are long gone. Every office and organisation now use laptops and desktops. These machines come equipped with
keyboards. Now for a person who likes to type without having to look at the keyboard every now and then, it becomes very difficult
with the default keyboards.

However, I was not aware of the types of keyboards that are present in the market. So, after suffering from this issue for quite some
time, I went into research mode.

## Research

My research led me down the rabbit hole. I was looking for a keyboard which was similar to the keyboard that I had seen being used
by IBM people, the PS/2 connectors, the clunky ones. For some reason, the sound was also something that I wanted to hear. I got to
know that the keyboards which give the feedback and also make the sound are called mechanical keyboards. They are all inspired from
the IBM PS/2 keyboards.

Upon further analysis, I found out that the sound as well as the feedback are controlled by something called switches. There are
different types of switches, Blue - the loudest, Brown - the medium one and Red - the quietest one. I was looking for a keyboard
which had Blue switches. I learnt about Cherry MX Blue switches. DAS Keyboard was something that was becoming popular at that time.
I thought of acquiring one, however the price of the keyboard stopped me dead in my tracks. So the search continued.

Post this affordability issue, I came to know about Kailh. They are a Chinese company which makes switches and they have a very
good quality of switches. They are also much more affordable than Cherry MX. I went from the Redgear MK881 keyboard. I think it came
with the Kailh Blue switches.

The day I got the keyboard, I kept on using it for the entire night. Coming from membrane keyboards, using this keyboard felt like
heaven. I used the keyboard for almost ~5 years. It was a great one, however the usage started to show its effect. The backlight of
some of the keys started to go off. It did not bother me as much since by that time I had kind of gotten accustomed to typing almost
on muscle memory.

The problem started when my better half told me that the keyboard made too much sound. Since I had gotten accustomed to hearing the
sound, it did not occur to me nor did it bother me. However, to a person who is looking at mechanical keyboards for the first time,
the sound was too much.

I started again looking at keyboards in which I would get the same feedback but much less sound. I also thought of going a bit on the
premium side this time. It is at this time, my research led me to Keychron.

## Keychron K2 V2


My Redgear MK881 was a full size keyboard. It was okay, but this time I wanted a keyboard which was having only the keys that I used the
most. My main usage of the keyboard had now confined to the home row, the space key and sometimes the arrow keys. For gaming as well, since
I play Dota(for just passing time and entertainment), I did not require a full-size keyboard. I settled on the K2-V2 with the brown switches.

The only thing this time I changed was that I selected white backlight. The MK881 had RGB backlight and it was something that I never liked.

I ordered the selected keyboard and till date I am using the same keyboard for all my purposes. I think I never mention how much I get stifled
when I am working in office since there I am not using a K2-V2, I am again using a Logitech or Dell membrane keyboard.

I have also used the Apple magic keyboard, but there is no keyboard which gives the feeling as that of a mechanical keyboard.

With that being said, I will probably stop ranting about keyboards and get back to writing code. If any of you stumble upon this post, give
the Keychron K2-V2 a try, you might like it.
